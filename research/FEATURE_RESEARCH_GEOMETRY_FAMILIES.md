# Circuit geometry, entanglement, and algorithm-family features

This note prioritizes additions to the Quantum Rings runtime predictor. The target is wall-clock time for an OpenQASM circuit at threshold 16, 64, or 512, under a 15-second per-circuit prediction budget. The challenge allows algorithm information inferred from circuit structure, but not source labels or scrambled filenames as predictors. Its description calls threshold an internal efficiency/accuracy control; it does **not** specify that the three challenge values are exact maximum MPS bond dimensions. Physics-inspired cost formulas below are therefore hypotheses to validate against the challenge labels, not statements about this simulator's implementation.

## Why the current representation loses signal

The model already uses qubit and gate counts, depth, interaction graph summaries, gate/angle diversity, temporal windows, a cut-based upper bound on `log2(χ)`, and an effective-χ interpolation. Its upper bound is a rank *possibility*, not the Schmidt rank actually reached. Under the current accounting, **447 of 532 circuits reach the dimension cap on every cut**. A feature based on the final upper bound alone then cannot tell apart a circuit that reaches it near the beginning and keeps applying costly gates from one that reaches it on the last layer. The existing circuit-grouped validation is about 0.8921 score and the harder structural-cluster holdout about 0.7005; these are baselines for the proposed ablations, not evidence that any new feature will improve the hidden score. See [χ/randomness evaluation](CHI_RANDOMNESS_EVALUATION.md).

The dense statevector is a separate reference scale: a complex64 vector of `n` qubits has `2^n` amplitudes and takes `8 × 2^n` bytes, before workspace or overhead. At 34 qubits this is 128 GiB, already beyond the stated 128 GB machine's nominal capacity. In this dataset, 309/532 circuits have more than 34 registered qubits and 275/532 have a connected interaction component larger than 34. This tells us a full dense statevector is not a universal work model; it does **not** prove which representation Quantum Rings uses for any particular circuit. [Qiskit Aer MPS documentation](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-aer/tutorials/7_matrix_product_state_method.html) explains the contrasting local-tensor representation and the costs of bond growth, singular-value decomposition, and nonlocal interactions.

## Highest-priority experiments

| Feature group | Concrete QASM-derived quantities | Why it could help | Extraction cost / caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time-resolved cut pressure** | For each cut and selected gate/layer checkpoints, cumulative crossing-gate rank budget, capped by `min(k,n-k)`; first 25/50/90% saturation point; area under capped cut-load curve; number of subsequent two-qubit gates after saturation; middle/peak cut trajectories. | Simulation work depends on intermediate tensor sizes at the gates that use them. It distinguishes early from late growth after the final χ bound saturates. | One streaming pass plus compact per-cut state. Gate rank bounds remain loose, especially for cancellation, commuting or special inputs. |
| **Alternative qubit orderings** | Interaction-edge-weighted cutwidth, mean/max crossing count, bandwidth and edge span in original order and reverse Cuthill–McKee (RCM) order; gain from reordering; sparse-vs-grid-vs-expander proxies. | MPS-style costs depend strongly on the qubit order. The order-independent interaction graph alone hides whether many interactions cross a 1D cut. | Build weighted graph from distinct pairs, then use cheap ordering heuristics. Do not assume the backend actually applies RCM. |
| **Algorithm motif scores** | Soft QFT, random-grid, QAOA/Ising, Grover, arithmetic, variational, GHZ/graph-state, QPE and repeated-block scores, plus an `unknown`/low-confidence score. | Family-specific structure can explain why equal-width/equal-depth circuits have different entanglement and simplification behavior. | Derive from operations and their order, never names or source IDs. Start with transparent rules; a classifier is optional after ablation. |

For the first group, let `B_k(t)` be the cumulative log-rank budget of gates up through checkpoint `t` that cross cut `k`; let `C_k = min(k,n-k)`. The inexpensive trajectory is `U_k(t)=min(B_k(t),C_k)`. Useful summaries include the earliest `t` at which `U_k(t)` reaches 25%, 50%, or 90% of `C_k`, and the mean of `U_k(t)` over two-qubit gate positions. This is an **upper-bound trajectory**, not measured entanglement. A threshold-conditioned proxy could aggregate `min(2^{U_k(t)}, τ)^p` over gates for several candidate `τ,p`, but should be learned or calibrated rather than treating challenge threshold as `τ` without evidence. [Vidal's MPS analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0301063) relates entanglement to simulation resources; [Markov and Shi](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0511069) relate circuit tensor-network contraction cost to graph width.

For ordering, compare original, reversed, RCM, degree-based, and perhaps a small beam search on the qubit interaction graph. Record the best and worst *weighted* cut-load profile, since repeated edges matter for runtime. In a second phase, build a reduced gate-time tensor-network graph and approximate elimination/contraction width with min-fill or greedy contraction. The qubit interaction graph's treewidth is **not** the circuit tensor network's exact contraction treewidth. [Markov and Shi](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0511069) provide the width rationale; [Ibrahim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02895) study contraction-tree optimization. A directly relevant [Quantum Rings family-aware study](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11620v1) already used RCM-derived cut and span features, but on a much smaller, different 36-circuit runtime evaluation.

Motifs should be soft scores rather than a hard family label. Candidate recognizers:

| Motif | Structural evidence to score | Distinguishing detail |
|---|---|---|
| QFT/inverse QFT | H plus triangular controlled-phase angle ladder and optional final bit reversal | CP count alone also appears in phase estimation. The [QFT small-entanglement result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08468) explicitly distinguishes the core transform from bit reversal. |
| Random grid / Sycamore-like | alternating single-qubit mixing and grid matching entangling layers, diverse angles/pairs | These circuits may approach high entanglement, but randomness must be inferred from the gates, not the source collection. |
| QAOA/Ising/Trotter | repeated edge sets with ZZ/controlled-phase rotations and mixer layers | Layer recurrence and parameter-sharing may matter more than gate count. |
| Grover/arithmetic/QPE | multi-control oracle and diffusion blocks; Toffoli/T-rich arithmetic; controlled powers followed by inverse QFT | Mixed circuits need multiple scores, not a forced single class. |
| GHZ/graph state/variational | H plus CX fanout; H plus CZ graph; repeated parameterized rotation/entangler ansatz | Graph-state preparation can have many edges yet strong structure. |

The [June 2026 Quantum Rings study](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11620v1) used QFT, arithmetic, and QNN fingerprints and a family-conditioned predictor, reporting a 3.2 percentage-point threshold-classification ablation gain in *its* data. Its 97.5% family-classifier validation used separately synthesized examples and its runtime evaluation only 36 distinct circuits. Neither number predicts improvement for this 532-circuit challenge. Use its features as candidates, not as a transfer guarantee. [MQT Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13719) is useful for defining possible algorithm families, though provenance labels must stay out of inference.

## Second-wave and speculative features

1. **Active-state and liveness profile.** Track the first/last active gate on each qubit, maximum simultaneous active width, retained ancilla duration, measurement/reset timing, and component sizes over time. Current counts omit their placement. [SupermarQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11045) characterizes circuits through communication, entanglement, parallelism, critical depth, and liveness; [QASMBench](https://doi.org/10.1145/3550488) includes density, retention lifespan, measurement density, and entanglement variance. Only seven of our circuits have a reset, so reset-specific features may be too sparse to matter.
2. **Post-simplification work.** Estimate cancellation of adjacent inverse gates, zero-angle gates, diagonal commutation, and recurring blocks; record raw-to-simplified gate/depth ratios. The current parser already detects some zero/small angles and cancellations, so this needs a measured ablation. The [11 September 2026 transpilation-aware study](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12980v1) supports the general importance of transformed circuit features, but it studies noisy Qiskit Aer, not this runtime target.
3. **Statevector regime and memory pressure.** Add `n+3` as `log2(dense_bytes)`, memory margin versus 128 GB, and the same estimate for the largest connected component. This is a routing/regime proxy only; it should never be interpreted as actual allocated memory.
4. **Entanglement spectrum and effective numerical rank.** For Schmidt probabilities `p_i` at a cut, `χ_ε = min{r : Σ_{i≤r} p_i ≥ 1-ε}` and `2^{S₂}=1/(Σ_i p_i²)` are better descriptions of compressibility than rank alone. They cannot generally be determined cheaply from QASM. A small exact light-cone or low-width pilot simulation could estimate them for selected circuits if it remains within 15 seconds; otherwise use the cut trajectory and motifs as proxies. [Vidal](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0301063) and [Qiskit Aer MPS documentation](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-aer/tutorials/7_matrix_product_state_method.html) give the underlying Schmidt/MPS motivation.
5. **Clifford/non-Clifford concentration.** T count, T-depth, and Clifford-run length can matter greatly for stabilizer-based simulators; [Bravyi and Gosset](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.07601) show a T-count-dependent simulation strategy. Since this challenge has not established that Quantum Rings uses that strategy, this is lower priority than geometry and timing.

## Validation and implementation order

First add **time-resolved cut summaries and RCM/original-order comparisons** to the existing streaming parser, retaining a fast fallback and missingness indicator for the exceptionally large QASM files. Then ablate **soft motif scores** separately and together. Only consider a family-conditioned model if the scores help on validation; otherwise retain the current regressor. A compact gate-time contraction-width heuristic can follow if the cheap graph features show value.

Keep all thresholds of a circuit in one fold. Compare with the existing circuit-grouped score and structural-cluster holdout, then inspect out-of-fold error by motif, qubit size, threshold, timeout status, and cut saturation. If family labels can be assigned independently for evaluation, hold out entire families to test whether motif features merely memorize a source distribution. Compare bootstrapped score differences and per-circuit extraction latency; the scoring submission must remain under 15 seconds on the largest files. Neither source filenames nor benchmark collection IDs should enter the model.

The likely first useful experiment is a **cut-load timeline × qubit-ordering** ablation. It addresses the specific failure of saturated final χ while staying cheap and physically interpretable. Algorithm recognition is the next experiment; it can capture structure that cut bounds miss, but needs stricter out-of-family testing.
