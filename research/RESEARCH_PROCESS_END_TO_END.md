# Quantum Rings runtime predictor: research process and decisions

This is the end-to-end record of the **experiment families** tried for the released Quantum Rings Quantathon v3 data. Individual grid settings, per-row predictions, and exact seeds live in the linked scripts/JSON/CSVs. The selected result is [v7](FINAL_MODEL_AND_FEATURES.md), a dual-QASM-scanner, χ-walk, threshold-expert ExtraTrees system with narrow validated rules; [the remaining misses](FINAL_FAILURE_MODES.md) are documented separately. These are released-label validation results, not hidden-holdout scores.

## Problem and evaluation contract

The [official challenge repository](https://github.com/Quantum-Rings/quantumrings-challenge-quantathonv3) supplies 532 scrambled QASM circuits and **1,497** labeled circuit/setting runs: 532 at threshold 16, 530 at 64, and 435 at 512, including **33 explicit timeouts**. The 99 unavailable combinations are missing labels, **not** assumed timeouts. Every run uses the same stated backend, shots, and precision. The harness asks for all **1,596** circuit/threshold combinations, with separate 15-second limits for parsing and inference.

We used the executable challenge scorer as the authority. For a success, the target is measured seconds; for a timeout, it is 14,400 seconds and predictions above that cap receive full timeout credit. Per-row score is `max(0, 1 − |log10(effective_prediction / target)| / 2)`: 10× error earns **0.5**, 100× error earns zero. The challenge prose at one point said otherwise. A successful 42,993.98-second label remains **above** the timeout cap and was preserved. Threshold response is empirically nonmonotone, so we did not impose increasing runtime with threshold. The [initial audit](PROPOSED_APPROACH.md) and [handoff](COMPARISON_HANDOFF.md) record those checks.

The principal model-selection split is five-fold **distribution-matched and circuit-grouped**: every threshold of a circuit, plus identical label-free circuit signatures, stays in one fold, and label-free structural clusters are spread across folds. A second five-fold **structural-cluster stress** split withholds coarse circuit families to expose transfer risk. Feature selection, fitted estimators, runtime-reference banks, and training-label analogues use only each training fold; label-free QASM features can be cached for the full corpus. Source filenames only join records and define groups. We also tested selected candidates on fresh grouped assignments. Repeated design choices on the same released labels create **selection bias**; a bootstrap interval reported within a probe is not a hidden-test guarantee.

## Engineering and operational checks

Early union parsing exceeded the 15-second limit on **nine** circuits (maximum 38.32 s). The large-file secondary scanner was changed from many whole-text scans to a single coarse count pass. The primary parser's hot path was optimized without changing its structural outputs. The selected v7 harness then produced **1,596/1,596** positive predictions with no cap violation; parser max **13.3033 s**, prediction max **0.0935 s**. The four QASM strings over 40 million decoded characters use a coarse fast path. [Large-file results](BIG_CIRCUITS_EVALUATION.md), [edge-case update](EDGE_CASE_UPDATE.md), [parser optimization](PARSER_OPTIMIZATION.md), and [holdout readiness](HOLDOUT_READINESS.md) provide the detailed timings and limitations.

The final 325-column artifact, reproducible feature catalog, strict submission validator, and read-only holdout inspector are separate from the rejected family/neighbor experiments. The inspector reports unusual geometry and nearest **missed/close** training analogues without changing predictions. All 38 tests passed after the paper runtime experiment; the saved artifact hash remained fixed. The later [failure audit](FINAL_FAILURE_MODES.md) shows why further hand-written rules have little obvious headroom: **28** remaining >10× matched-fold rows, but loss spread across many circuit types and dominated by successful rather than timed-out runs.

## How the papers informed the work

- [Azizov, Vela-Tambo, and Guo, *Transpilation-Aware Runtime Prediction for Noisy Quantum Circuit Simulation* (11 September 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12980): motivated raw versus transformed-work features and keeping ordinary regressors competitive with graph models. Its Qiskit Aer/noisy-backend context and post-transpilation graphs are **not** the Quantum Rings execution trace; we used bounded source-QASM approximations instead.
- [Vidal, *Efficient classical simulation of slightly entangled quantum computations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0301063), [Markov and Shi, *Simulating quantum computation by contracting tensor networks*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0511069), and [Ibrahim et al., *Constructing Optimal Contraction Trees for Tensor Network Quantum Circuit Simulation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02895): motivate χ/cut and graph-width proxies. Our static interaction graph and walk are **not** realized Schmidt ranks or optimal contraction trees.
- [Aaronson and Gottesman, *Improved Simulation of Stabilizer Circuits*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406196): motivates Clifford/non-Clifford composition, but we do not claim this backend invokes a stabilizer simulator.
- [SupermarQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11045) and [QASMBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13018): supplied structural-feature vocabulary such as communication, liveness, depth, density, and measurement. These benchmark features are descriptive, not calibrated runtime equations.
- [MQT Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13719), the original [QAOA paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.4028), and [Chen et al. on low-entanglement core QFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08468): informed generated family references and interpretation of the QAOA/QFT error cases. Generated-family recognition did not establish the challenge circuits' true algorithms.
- [Xing et al., *Family-Aware Residual Architecture for Predicting Quantum Circuit Simulation Performance* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11620): motivated the later independent runtime-only FiLM/residual test. The original paper's threshold and runtime results concern a different 36-circuit, multi-context sample; our grouped challenge test found no robust family gain.

The papers supplied plausible mechanisms and candidate representations. **Local, circuit-grouped score changes and runtime-budget checks** determined what entered the selected predictor. Rebuild and run commands are in the [final model](FINAL_MODEL_AND_FEATURES.md) and [holdout guide](HOLDOUT_READINESS.md).
